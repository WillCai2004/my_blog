Byte Budget

The length limit for the shellcode is only 0x12 = 18 bytes. Also, the first page of the shellcode is not writable. Therefore, we cannot have a full open/read/write . We will have to implement two stages. However, since the first page is not writable, and the stack is not executable by default, none of the obvious two-stage approaches work. This points us in the direction of SROP (Sigreturn Oriented Programming).

⚠️ Still Not Yet Solved End-to-End. There is an outline of our approach and a confirmed stage0. Stage2 is still under development.

# 1. Problem Mechanics

• Input is only 18 bytes long.
• The first page of the shellcode is not writable.
• The stack is not executable by default.

# 2. Failed Approaches

• Two-stage, reading stage2 back into the shellcode page: The first page was not writable. I could not write to it.
• Read stage2 onto the stack and jmp rsp : Stack was not executable. Jumping there caused a SIGSEGV.

# 3. Right Direction: SROP

18 bytes are not enough space to contain all the logic, and the pages were restricted in what they could be written to, so we use rt_sigreturn to restore all of our registers at once:

  1. An 18-byte stage0: read(0, rsp, 0x400) reads a fabricated sigreturn frame onto the stack;
  2. stage0 calls rt_sigreturn ;
  3. the fabricated frame configures our registers to call mprotect to make our shellcode/page either executable or writable;
  4. jump to the actual stage2;
  5. stage2 calls open/read/write on /flag .

# 4. Status / To Do

  • stage0 is short enough (18 bytes), can read + call rt_sigreturn .
  • Incomplete: The specific values in our fabricated sigreturn frame ( rsp/rip/rdi/rsi/rdx/rax , mprotect args, page-aligned addresses) need to be mapped out relative to struct rt_sigframe / ucontext and connected to where stage2 wants to run.

# 5. Takeaways (to date)

  • Double constraint of page perms + limited size → SROP: When your shellcode page is not writable, your stack is not executable, and your byte budget is small, rt_sigreturn (syscall 15) is how you get to set "every register in one syscall" — typically to call mprotect and gain execute permission.
  • Shortest possible stage0 template: read(0,rsp,N) + rt_sigreturn , with our fake sigframe directly behind it on the stack — reachable within those 18 bytes.
  • More to do: offset values for the fake sigframe and alignment/jump for mprotect(addr & ~0xfff,len,7) .
